# Rights Protocol

**Status:** Phase Zero foundational draft

**Authority:** Downstream of [`MOOD_CANON.md`](../../MOOD_CANON.md)

## Abstract

Rights define what a participant is permitted to access, influence, receive, use, or claim within a specific coordination context.

MOOD does not assume that every contribution creates the same right. Rights must arise from explicit policy, relevant evidence, and a legitimate decision process.

## Rights Categories

A project or protocol module may define rights such as:

- Access rights
- Participation rights
- Attribution rights
- Usage or licensing rights
- Governance rights
- Revenue or settlement rights
- Stewardship rights
- Appeal and dispute rights

These categories are conceptual and do not themselves create legal, financial, ownership, or governance entitlements.

## From Contribution to Rights

```text
CONTRIBUTION
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
VERIFICATION
  ↓
APPLICABLE RIGHTS POLICY
  ↓
RIGHT GRANT, DENIAL, OR DISPUTE
  ↓
RECORDED OUTCOME
```

Contribution is necessary evidence in many cases, but it is not by itself a right grant. The applicable policy must define thresholds, scope, duration, transferability, revocation, conflicts, and appeal.

## Boundaries

- Reputation is evidence of historical contribution, not an automatic bundle of rights.
- Capital is one contribution type and does not automatically create superior authority.
- Tokens do not automatically establish legitimacy or governance rights.
- Organization membership and project participation may create different rights and responsibilities.
- Rights claims must distinguish protocol recognition from rights enforceable under applicable law.
- Irreversible grants, treasury movement, deployment, or public activation require explicit human approval where applicable.

## Principle

Rights must be legible, scoped, evidence-based, and contestable. No participant should gain hidden authority through an undefined conversion from contribution, reputation, capital, or technical control.
