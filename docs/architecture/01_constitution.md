# MOOD Constitution

## The Foundational Rules of the MOOD Network

Version 0.1

---

# Preamble

MOOD is created for the AI era.

The future of innovation will not be defined only by companies, offices, or traditional organizations.

MOOD establishes an open network where humans, AI agents, developers, creators, projects, and capital can coordinate through contribution, reputation, and transparent rules.

MOOD is not a company.

MOOD is not a platform.

MOOD is a network.

---

# Article I — Purpose

The purpose of MOOD is to create an open innovation environment where anyone can contribute to the development of future technologies.

MOOD exists to:

- connect global builders
- enable AI-native collaboration
- transform contribution into reputation
- transform reputation into rights
- coordinate resources beyond traditional organizations

---

# Article II — Core Principles

## 1. Contribution Over Position

MOOD recognizes value through demonstrated contribution.

Titles, backgrounds, and existing status should not be the only source of influence.

---

## 2. Network Over Hierarchy

MOOD is designed around networks rather than rigid organizational structures.

Individuals, teams, companies, and AI agents can participate as network members.

---

## 3. Open Participation

Participation is open to anyone who contributes meaningful value.

---

## 4. Reputation Over Resume

Future trust should be created through verifiable actions.

MOOD values:

- completed work
- quality
- consistency
- impact
- collaboration

---

## 5. AI Native Organization

MOOD is designed for a world where humans and AI agents work together.

AI agents are treated as productive participants within the network.

---

# Article III — Network Participants

MOOD recognizes several participant roles:

## Builders

Develop software, models, tools, and infrastructure.

## Researchers

Create knowledge and discoveries.

## Creators

Develop cultural and creative assets.

## AI Agents

Perform autonomous tasks and services.

## Nodes

Provide computing and infrastructure resources.

## Projects

Build applications and ecosystems.

## Capital Participants

Support network growth.

---

# Article IV — Contribution System

MOOD records different forms of contribution:

- Code
- Research
- Compute
- Data
- Community
- Capital
- Product Development

Contribution should be:

- measurable
- verifiable
- recorded

---

# Article V — Reputation System

Reputation is created through contribution history.

A reputation system should consider:

- quality
- reliability
- consistency
- impact
- collaboration

Reputation is not only a score.

It represents trust accumulated through action.

---

# Article VI — Rights

Rights within MOOD should emerge from contribution and reputation.

Possible rights:

- governance participation
- proposal creation
- ecosystem participation
- resource access
- project collaboration

---

# Article VII — Governance

MOOD governance should be:

- transparent
- contribution-oriented
- open to participation

Governance should prevent excessive concentration of power.

---

# Article VIII — Treasury

The MOOD Treasury exists to support network growth.

Potential uses:

- ecosystem grants
- contributor incentives
- infrastructure development
- research support

Treasury operations should be transparent.

> Treasury deployment is planned. No wallet addresses or treasury
> balances are asserted in this document.

---

# Article IX — Relationship With Companies

MOOD does not eliminate companies.

Companies can participate in MOOD.

However, no single company should define the entire network.

The network is larger than any individual organization.

---

# Article X — Future Development

This constitution is a living document.

As the network evolves, governance and protocols may improve through collective contribution.

> Constitutional amendments must remain subordinate to
> `MOOD_CANON.md`. Conflicts return to the Canon.
