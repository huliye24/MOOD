# MOOD Core Lexicon

**Status:** Foundational Draft  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Authority:** Subordinate to `MOOD_CANON.md`

## Identity layers

### MOODISM

A contribution-driven vision of network civilization and the civilizational
philosophy of MOOD. It answers what kind of network society MOOD seeks; it does
not independently authorize protocol or economic mechanisms.

### MOOD

The independent project, open coordination world, and public name through
which MOODISM is explored. MOOD is not defined by one application, company,
website, or token.

### MOOD Protocol

The institutional and technical layer that translates stable canonical
concepts into rules for identity, contribution, proof, reputation, rights,
responsibility, coordination, governance, and settlement.

### Moodify

A historical origin and potential ecosystem application. It may participate
in or build on MOOD, but it does not define MOOD.

## Core concepts

### Actor

A human, machine, organization, project, or infrastructure node capable of
meaningful action within an explicit accountability boundary.

### Contribution

An action, outcome, or resource commitment that creates identifiable value for
the network, a community, or public memory. A contribution claim is not proof
that a contribution has been established.

### Contribution Evidence

Inspectable material, records, or reproducible results supporting a
contribution claim.

### Contribution Proof

The result of verifying contribution evidence under explicit rules. Proof of
authenticity does not automatically establish quality, impact, or reward.

### Reputation

A multidimensional trust record formed from auditable behavioral history. It
is not a single score, a purchasable title, or permanent authority.

### Rights

Capabilities to act, participate, appeal, exit, or access that are protected
by foundational principles or explicit rules. Fundamental rights are not
rewards.

### Interests / Entitlements

Non-economic or economic eligibility created by explicit institutions. An
entitlement is not automatically ownership and must not arise directly from an
ambiguous contribution claim.

### Responsibility

The traceable obligations an actor bears for conduct, impact, resource use,
and granted authority. Greater influence should require greater accountability.

### Ownership

A legitimate relation of control, use, or disposition over assets, outcomes,
or institutional resources. Contribution, reputation, governance authority,
and ownership require separate definitions.

### Digital Resources

Computation, storage, bandwidth, data, models, knowledge, code, digital assets,
and other identifiable resources that a network may coordinate.

### Commons

Knowledge, infrastructure, relationships, or public memory maintained in
common and not presumed to be the private property of one platform actor.

### Network Civilization

A durable digital order formed by shared meaning, culture, rules, memory,
rights, responsibilities, and institutions of coordination—not merely a market
or software product.

## Distinctions that must remain explicit

```text
Contribution ≠ Contribution Evidence
Contribution Evidence ≠ Contribution Proof
Proof ≠ Quality
Quality ≠ Long-term Impact
Reputation ≠ Governance Authority
Governance Authority ≠ Ownership
Ownership ≠ Token Holdings
Contract Existence ≠ Active Protocol Economy
```
